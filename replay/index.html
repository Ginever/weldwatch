Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, tap to jump. Zoom far enough and you see every individual sample, 3 µs apart. Camera 15 fps; electrical data 333,333 samples/s per channel.

What am I looking at?

The top trace is arc voltage, the bottom arc current. Zoomed out, each pixel shows the min–max range of thousands of samples, so the band's thickness is the fast ripple. Zoom to 1 ms or less and the ripple resolves, sample by sample, into the welder's 49.9 kHz inverter cycle.

The square panel plots voltage against current for the samples on screen. A healthy arc traces the same loop every switching cycle; a damaged electrode smears it. Compare flat-tip with contaminated.

About this data

Recorded 11 September 2026 with WeldWatch on a TIG welder. Voltage = code × 20/4095 V. Current from a Hall clamp, calibrated against the welder's setpoints: I = (code − zero)/6.574 A. Rare single-sample glitches from the voltage ADC's receive interface (≈1 per run) are repaired by interpolation. Video and data both start at the moment recording begins; alignment is at frame level (±1/15 s). Run labels follow the operator's file names.
